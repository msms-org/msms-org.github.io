Maliyadeva Simulation & Modelling Society

Some systems are too big, too slow, or too dangerous to experiment on. So we build them instead.

Why does a traffic jam appear on an empty road? How fast does a rumour actually spread? What happens to a forest if you remove one species? You can't run those experiments in real life. You can run them in code.

Questions we chase

Where do traffic jams come from?

Not from crashes or bottlenecks — a jam can appear spontaneously on an empty road. Nagel–Schreckenberg cellular automaton.

How do ants find short paths without a map?

Individual ants follow extremely simple rules, yet colonies can collectively discover efficient routes. Swarm Intelligence.

How fast can a rumour spread?

Information can propagate through a population much like an infection, but human behaviour changes the dynamics. Diffusion.

Why is the other queue always faster?

It usually isn't — but the maths of waiting is genuinely counterintuitive. Queueing theory.

Do you need to be good at maths?

Not at all. You don't need to arrive knowing advanced maths — just be willing to learn. You'll encounter mathematics when it becomes useful, and you can learn it along the way. What matters most is curiosity, patience, and a willingness to figure things out.

You don't need to be a programmer

Half the club started with no coding experience. If you can write a for-loop, you're ready. If you can't yet, come anyway — the first session is designed for you, and modelling is a genuinely good way to learn to code, because every line you write does something visible.

BYLAWS
Maliyadeva Simulation and Modelling Society (MSMS)
Maliyadeva College

Article I - GENERAL PROVISIONS

Section 1. Definitions

For these Bylaws, unless the context otherwise requires:

  1. College: Maliyadeva College.

  2. Executive Committee (EC): the executive body, established under Article IV Section 2 of the Constitution.

  3. General Assembly (GA): the body established under Article IV, Section 1 of the Constitution.

  4. Teacher-in-Charge (TIC): the office established under Article IV, Section 3 of the Constitution.

  5. Good Standing: the status of a member who has met all registration requirements and is not suspended or under disciplinary sanction.

Article II - MEMBERSHIP

Section 1. Admission

Any eligible student of Maliyadeva College may apply for Associate Membership by submitting the prescribed membership application form. Admission takes effect when Secretary records the new member in the Register.

To be eligible for admission, an applicant must:

  1. Be a currently enrolled student of Maliyadeva College.

  2. Agree to abide by these Bylaws, the Constitution, and applicable College policies.

Section 2. Progression to Regular Membership

An Associate Member may apply for promotion to Regular Membership through either of the following pathways:

  1. Project Contribution Pathway: The member’s technical contribution is verified and signed off by the Director of Technical Affairs and Projects.

  2. Competition Pathway: The member’s active participation as an official contestant in an EC-approved competition is verified by the relevant Team Leader or the Director of Competitions and Events.

Section 3. Responsibilities

Every member of the Society shall:

  1. Compliance: Comply with these Bylaws and all applicable policies of Maliyadeva College.

  2. Conduct: Act in accordance with the Society’s Code of Conduct.

  3. Participation: Fulfill commitments undertaken for Society projects, teams, or committees.

  4. Integrity: Maintain academic and technical integrity in all Society work.

  5. Resource Management: Use Society and College resources responsibly.

  6. Representation: Refrain from improperly misrepresenting the Society or its positions.

  7. Cooperation: Cooperate with legitimate Society procedures and officer decisions.

Article III - GENERAL ASSEMBLY

Section 1. Meetings

The Society shall hold an Annual General Meeting (AGM) to elect officers in each academic year, review executive and financial reports, and establish priorities for the upcoming year.

Extraordinary meetings may be called by a resolution of the EC or a written petition signed by at least one-third of the Regular Members. The President, or the Vice President in their absence, shall preside over the meeting.

Section 2. Quorum

A quorum consists of a simple majority of all Regular Members in good standing. If a quorum is not met, the meeting shall be adjourned and rescheduled by the EC.

Section 3. Voting

Each Regular Member is entitled to one vote on any matter submitted to the General Assembly. Associate and Honorary Members do not possess voting rights.

Section 4. Resolutions

Any Regular Member may submit a motion for consideration by providing it in writing to the Secretary before the meeting. The Secretary shall circulate any such motions to Regular Members as soon as reasonably practicable. Such motions shall be decided by a simple majority vote, provided they do not attempt to bypass formal amendment procedures for the Constitution, Bylaws.

Article IV - EXECUTIVE COMMITTEE

Section 1. Composition

The Executive Committee shall consist of:

  1. President;

  2. Vice President;

  3. Secretary;

  4. Treasurer;

  5. Director of Technical Affairs and Projects; and

  6. Director of Competitions and Events.

Section 2. Powers

The EC shall serve as the primary executive and operational body of the Society and shall have the authority to:

  1. Plan, organize, approve, and direct all Society operations, projects, competitions, publications, and events.

  2. Establish ad-hoc committees, project teams, and competition squads, delegating specific operational duties as required. Where an officer is temporarily unavailable or unable to perform a duty, the EC may temporarily delegate that duty to another officer or eligible member, subject to the limits of the delegated authority.

  3. Manage Society funds, physical assets, and membership records in accordance with the approved budget, these Bylaws, and College policies.

  4. Enforce compliance with the Constitution, Bylaws, and Code of Conduct, exercising ordinary administrative authority not specifically reserved for the General Assembly.

Section 3. Meetings

The EC shall meet regularly during the academic year. Regular meetings shall be convened by the Secretary in consultation with the President.

  1. Notice: The Secretary shall issue a meeting notice and agenda at least 24 hours in advance.

  2. Chairing: Meetings shall be chaired by the President or, in their absence, the Vice President.

  3. Minutes: The Secretary shall record minutes, attendance, and resolutions.

The EC may act by written resolution without a meeting. A written resolution requires the approval of three members, or a majority of seated members where fewer than five are seated, and must be approved by the President or the Secretary. Approval may be given electronically, provided the Secretary records each approving member and the result. Informal discussion, including by electronic means, is not a decision of the EC.

Section 4. Quorum

A quorum is three officers, or a majority of seated officers where fewer than five are seated, and must include the President or the Secretary. If a quorum is not present within a reasonable time, or is lost during a meeting, the meeting shall be adjourned.

Section 5. Vacancies

An elective office that becomes vacant shall be filled by by-election at an Extraordinary General Meeting, or at the next Annual General Meeting if that falls within 60 days. Pending the filling of the vacancy, the Executive Committee may appoint an eligible Regular Member as Acting Officer.

An appointed office that becomes vacant shall be filled by the Executive Committee for the remainder of the term. If the Presidency becomes vacant, the Vice President shall serve as Acting President.

An Acting Officer serves until a successor assumes office and may exercise only the ordinary powers of that office.

Article V - OFFICERS

Section 1. President

The President shall serve as the chief executive of the Society, with the responsibility and authority to:

  1. Leadership and Coordination: Provide overall direction and leadership to the Society, coordinate the work of the EC, promote effective cooperation, direct officers in the implementation of their duties, and assign temporary operational tasks within the limits of these Bylaws.

  2. Meetings and Implementation: Preside over meetings of the General Assembly and EC, coordinate the implementation of duly adopted decisions, and address urgent operational matters where immediate action is reasonably necessary.

  3. Representation: Represent the Society in official dealings with the College, external organisations, sponsors, and partners, and in routine official matters within the authority granted by these Bylaws or the EC, subject to applicable College policies.

  4. Oversight and Reporting: Monitor the overall progress of Society programmes, projects, and competitions; request necessary reports or information from officers and committees; and present, or cause to be presented, the Annual Executive Report and Annual Financial Report to the Annual General Meeting.

Section 2. Secretary

The Secretary shall oversee the administration, procedural compliance, and official records of the Society, with the responsibility and authority to:

  1. Meetings and Communications: Coordinate the convening of meetings, issue required notices, prepare agendas, receive motions, ensure applicable notice periods are observed, and advise the General Assembly and EC on procedural requirements. Conduct and issue official administrative communications, notices, and decisions authorised by the GA or EC.

  2. Administrative Authority and Records: Exercise authority over official administrative affairs, correspondence, and procedural processes. Maintain official copies of the Constitution, Bylaws, Regulations, and membership/officer registers. Certify copies or extracts of Society records as true records, and support the orderly transfer of accounts, access, and ongoing administrative matters between administrations.

  3. Oversight and Compliance: Facilitate the communication and implementation of decisions. Request from any officer or committee information reasonably necessary for record-keeping, administration, or determining procedural compliance. Require that a proposed or adopted EC decision be referred back for review where the Secretary reasonably believes it is inconsistent with governing instruments or College policy.

  4. Limitations: The Secretary shall not, solely by virtue of office, alter the substance of a resolution or decision when recording or issuing it, nor refuse to record or communicate a lawful decision solely because the Secretary disagrees with it.

Section 3. Vice President

The Vice President shall support the general administration of the Society, with the responsibility and authority to:

  1. Executive Support and Implementation: Assist the President in executive leadership, monitor the implementation and progress of EC decisions and projects, and exercise authority delegated by the President or EC within the scope of that delegation.

  2. Coordination and Programmes: Facilitate coordination among officers and project teams, and lead major initiatives assigned by the EC. Resolve ordinary coordination difficulties, referring substantive disputes, financial, or governance matters to the appropriate authority. Request reasonable progress updates concerning matters within their responsibilities.

  3. Acting Presidency: Act as President, and exercise the authority of the President, when the President is absent or unable to perform the functions of the office, subject to the same limitations and accountability applicable to the President.

Section 4. Treasurer

The Treasurer shall have authority over the financial administration and financial control of the Society, with the responsibility and authority to:

  1. Financial Records, Assets and Banking: Maintain accurate and up-to-date records of the Society’s income, expenditure, assets, liabilities, balances, and supporting documentation. Maintain or oversee the Society’s approved banking and accounting arrangements. At the end of the term, transfer all financial records, account information, and assets to the successor. Maintain a register of Society financial assets and property and coordinate with relevant officers regarding their custody and handover.

  2. Budgeting and Reporting: Prepare the annual budget in consultation with the EC, present to the General Assembly for approval, monitor actual expenditure against the approved budget, and prepare or review financial estimates for Society projects, competitions, workshops, events, and other programmes, and advise the EC on their financial feasibility. Prepare the annual Financial Report and bring suspected irregularities, unauthorised expenditure, significant budget deviations, and material financial risks to the attention of the EC.

  3. Funds and Expenditure Control: Receive or oversee the receipt of Society funds and ensure they are properly safeguarded. Require officers, committees, project teams, or persons incurring expenditure or handling Society funds to provide reasonably necessary financial information and supporting documentation.

  4. Oversight and Suspension Power: Verify that expenditure is properly authorised and supported. The Treasurer may refuse or suspend the processing of a financial transaction where there are reasonable grounds to believe that the transaction lacks required authorisation, exceeds an applicable financial limit, is inconsistent with an approved budget or governing instrument, or lacks required supporting documentation. The Treasurer shall decline to process such transactions and inform the relevant authority of the deficiency.

Section 5. Director of Technical Affairs and Projects

The Director of Technical Affairs and Projects shall oversee the technical and computational work of the Society, with the responsibility and authority to:

  1. Technical Direction and Planning: Set and guide the technical direction of Society projects in accordance with EC objectives. Determine the technical approach, establish work plans, milestones, and deadlines, and assign technical tasks within approved projects. Evaluate proposed technical projects and advise the EC on feasibility, requirements, risks, and resource needs.

  2. Project Coordination and Capability: Coordinate the Society’s modelling, simulation, computational, and programming projects with Project Leads. Identify technical skills required by members, facilitate training and knowledge transfer, and recommend or organise technical learning activities. Reorganise technical work where reasonably necessary, provided it does not alter an approved project’s material scope or financial commitments.

  3. Technical Standards and Assessment: Establish, maintain, and monitor technical standards. Review the technical quality of projects, models, code, and outputs. Assess and certify the technical readiness of a project or output. Require modification, correction, or further review of technical work that does not satisfy requirements, referring serious deficiencies to the EC.

  4. Infrastructure and Representation: Maintain, administer, and develop the Society’s website, source repositories, deployment configuration, and technical infrastructure (coordinating content with the Communications Officer). Represent the Society in technical matters when authorised.

  5. Limitations: The Director’s technical authority shall not extend to authorising any financial commitment, institutional obligation, external commitment, or other matter reserved to the EC, General Assembly, or Treasurer.

Section 6. Director of Competitions and Events

The Director of Competitions and Events shall manage the operational execution of Society events, with the responsibility and authority to:

  1. Competitions and Events Administration: Coordinate the Society’s participation in external competitions and plan Society-organised events, exhibitions, and academic programmes. Identify and reporting suitable opportunities; Make operational arrangements, manage registrations, coordinate participants, travel, logistics, contingency plans, participation records, and post-event follow-up. Monitor practical execution and report operational issues to the appropriate authority.

  2. Operational Authority and Communication: Make ordinary administrative and operational decisions within the authority of the office without separate EC approval for each matter, provided they align with approved decisions, financial requirements, and College policies. Communicate and liaise with external organisers, venues, speakers, judges, and service providers for approved activities.

  3. Coordination and Limitations: Coordinate with the Director of Technical Affairs and Projects regarding technical requirements and readiness, but shall not exercise technical authority assigned to that office. The Director’s authority shall not extend to matters reserved to the GA, EC, Treasurer, Secretary, or other authorised bodies.

Section 7. General Duties and Accountability of Officers

7.1 General Duties

Every officer shall:

  1. attend Executive Committee meetings regularly, participate in decisions, and give reasonable notice when unable to attend;

  2. perform the duties of their office and exercise delegated authority with reasonable care, diligence, and judgement;

  3. comply with the Constitution, Bylaws, Regulations, and applicable College requirements;

  4. provide information and assistance reasonably required by other officers, coordinate where responsibilities overlap, and report matters requiring the attention of the Executive Committee; and

  5. maintain records for their portfolio and enable the handover of information, credentials, contacts, and ongoing work to a successor.

7.2 Accountability

Each officer is accountable to the Executive Committee for the performance of their duties and the exercise of their authority.

  1. Protection from Liability: An officer is not personally liable for any outcome, act, omission, error, or information arising from circumstances beyond their reasonable control, provided they have acted with reasonable care and in accordance with the governing instruments.

  2. Review of Decisions: Any decision of an officer may be reviewed by the Executive Committee, which may take any action within its authority.

  3. Acting Capacity: An officer designated to act in another office is accountable for that office’s duties and authority for the duration of the designation.

Section 8. Dual Office Holding

A member may hold at most two Executive Committee offices at once but has only one vote. No member may concurrently hold:

  1. the offices of President and Vice President;

  2. the offices of President and Secretary; or

  3. the office of Treasurer together with any other office.

Section 9. Term of Office

Officers serve until their successors take office, unless they resign, are removed, or otherwise cease to hold office earlier. An officer filling a vacancy serves the remainder of the original term.

Article VI - ELECTIONS AND APPOINTMENTS

Section 1. Elections

The Executive Committee shall be elected by the General Assembly. Candidates must be Regular Members in good standing, and shall be nominated, with their consent, by any eligible member or by themselves.

The Secretary shall verify eligibility; where the Secretary is a candidate, the Teacher-in-Charge (TIC) shall verify eligibility and oversee that election.

Elections shall be by secret ballot. Each eligible member present has one vote for each contested position.

  1. Uncontested positions are filled automatically.

  2. Votes shall be counted by two members appointed by the meeting. No candidate may count votes for a position they contest.

  3. The candidate with the most valid votes is elected.

  4. If a tie occurs, the matter shall be referred to the TIC.

Results shall be recorded in the minutes and reported to the TIC. Elected officers shall assume office upon election.

Section 2. Appointments

The Executive Committee may create and fill non-elected positions, including the following, as it considers necessary to conduct Society activities:

  1. Communications and Outreach Coordinator: responsible for the Society’s public channels, publicity, external enquiries, and member engagement; and

  2. Project Leads: each responsible for a project under the direction of the Director of Technical Affairs and Projects.

Appointees report to the Executive Committee, are not members of it, do not vote, and serve at the discretion of the Executive Committee.

Section 3. Removal from Office

An elected officer may be removed by the General Assembly for serious misconduct, substantial violation of Society or College policy, or persistent failure to perform their duties.

Disciplinary procedures under Article X shall apply.

Article VII - COMMITTEES AND SUBCOMMITTEES

The Executive Committee or the General Assembly may establish standing or ad-hoc committees by resolution to support the Society’s objectives and operations.

The establishing resolution shall define the committee’s name, purpose, delegated authority, duration, and reporting structure. Unless the resolution provides otherwise, the Executive Committee shall appoint the Chair and members, who may be Regular or Associate Members. Service on a committee does not confer voting rights.

A committee has only the authority granted in its establishing resolution. It has no independent governance authority, may not make external commitments, and may not authorise expenditure without the approval of the Executive Committee.

The Chair shall coordinate the committee’s work, keep records, report progress to the Executive Committee, and submit records to the Secretary. The Executive Committee may review, modify, or suspend any committee. A committee established by the Executive Committee may be dissolved by it; a committee established by the General Assembly may be dissolved only by the General Assembly

Article VIII - FINANCIAL ADMINISTRATION

Section 1. Society Funds and Banking

All Society funds shall be used only for its objectives and held in the account or arrangement designated by the College. No funds shall be held in any personal account.

Section 2. Budget and Expenditure

The Treasurer shall prepare an annual budget, approved by the General Assembly at the AGM and submitted to the Teacher-in-Charge and College as required.

No expenditure may be committed without prior approval. Expenditure within the approved budget is authorised by the Treasurer; anything outside it requires the approval of the Executive Committee and the Teacher-in-Charge, and any further College approval. A single expense shall not be split to avoid this requirement. The Executive Committee may approve reimbursement of expenses properly incurred, on production of receipts.

Section 3. Financial Records

The Treasurer shall keep records of all income, expenditure, and assets, present a summary to the Executive Committee each term and a report at the AGM, and make records available on request to the Teacher-in-Charge, the Executive Committee, and the College.

Article IX - ACTIVITIES AND SOCIETY PROPERTY

Section 1. Society Activities

The Society may organise or participate in any activity that advances its objectives. An activity within an officer’s delegated authority may be approved by that officer; any activity beyond that authority shall be approved by the Teacher-in-Charge. No commitment may be made on the assumption that College approval will be granted.

Section 2. Competitions and Projects

Competitions

The Society may establish teams to participate in external competitions. Where participation is limited, the Director of Competitions and Events shall establish fair, transparent selection criteria and oversee the selection process.

Projects

The Executive Committee may authorise project teams and shall designate a Project Lead. Any eligible member may join, subject to the Project Lead’s discretion and any conditions set by the Executive Committee.

Technical outputs published externally in the Society’s name are subject to review under procedures established by the Director of Technical Affairs and Projects.

Section 3. Use of Society Property and Identity

Equipment, funds, and other property acquired with Society funds shall belong to the Society. Personal-fund purchases for the Society shall be reimbursed and become Society property; if not reimbursed, they shall remain the member’s. Intellectual property shall remain with the creator. The Society may use project outputs for educational/institutional purposes, acknowledging contributors.

The Society’s name, logo, and identity shall be used only for authorised purposes; endorsement shall require Executive Committee approval.

On leaving office or completing a project, members shall transfer to their successor (or Secretary if none) all Society property, records, files, credentials, and access. Personal property and accounts shall be excluded.

Article X - DISCIPLINE AND ACCOUNTABILITY

All members and officers shall follow the Society’s Code of Conduct, along with Society rules and College policies. Complaints shall be reported to the TIC, who may warn, suspend privileges, recommend removal of an officer, or refer the matter to College authorities. TIC and College decisions shall be final. Good-faith accidental breaches of purely administrative rules shall not be disciplined.

Article XI - AMENDMENTS AND REGULATIONS

Section 1. Amending the Bylaws

Amendments to these Bylaws may be proposed by a resolution of the EC or by a written petition signed by at least 10 Regular Members, submitted to the Secretary.

Approval requires a simple majority vote of the Regular Members present and voting, provided a quorum is met.

Approved amendments shall take effect immediately upon the conclusion of the General Assembly, or a later specified date if applicable, shall remain subject to any required confirmation by the Teacher-in-Charge, and shall not operate retroactively.

Section 2. Regulations

Regulations may be adopted, amended, or repealed by a simple majority vote of the General Assembly, following a written proposal by the EC or at least five (5) Regular Members and a seven-day notice period. Regulations must not conflict with these Bylaws or College policy.

Article XII - FINAL PROVISIONS

Section 1. Interpretation

The Secretary is responsible for the interpretation of the governing documents of the Society.

If a ruling by the Secretary is disputed, or if an ambiguity cannot be reasonably resolved, the matter shall be referred to the EC for a final internal determination.

Section 2. Effect and Initial Adoption

These Bylaws shall take effect upon their approval by the EC and the Teacher-in-Charge pursuant to the transitional authority established under Section 10 of the Transitional Regulations.

Section 3. Supersession

Upon taking effect, these Bylaws supersede and replace all previous bylaws, regulations, and administrative rules of the Society.

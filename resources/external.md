# External resources

Books, videos, and courses we've found genuinely useful. Most of them
aren't about simulation directly — good modelling borrows from
mathematics, computer science, and physics, and the best way to get
better at it is to read widely.

## Books

- **[Think Complexity](https://greenteapress.com/wp/think-complexity-2e/)**
  — Allen Downey's free book on complexity science and computational
  modelling, written for people who can already write code.
- **[Think Stats](https://greenteapress.com/wp/think-stats-2e/)**
  — Downey again, on probability and statistics from a programmer's
  angle. Useful before you trust any model's output.
- **[Modeling and Simulation in Python](https://greenteapress.com/wp/modsimpy/)**
  — Downey's third. A guided tour of building models from first
  principles. Directly relevant to everything we do.
- **[Grokking Algorithms](https://www.manning.com/books/grokking-algorithms)**
  — Aditya Bhargava. Illustrated, gentle, and covers the algorithmic
  thinking that shows up everywhere in modelling.

## Videos and channels

- **[3Blue1Brown](https://www.3blue1brown.com/)** — visual intuition
  for the maths behind modelling: linear algebra, calculus, and
  probability, all made visual.
- **[Computerphile](https://www.youtube.com/user/Computerphile)** —
  short videos on computer science, often with a simulation or
  algorithms angle.
- **[Veritasium](https://www.youtube.com/user/1veritasium)** — science
  and mathematics explained through stories. Good for building
  intuition about the systems we model.

## Practice

- **[Project Euler](https://projecteuler.net/)** — mathematics problems
  designed for programming. Good practice for building computational
  intuition.
- **[Advent of Code](https://adventofcode.com/)** — annual programming
  puzzles, released every December. Great for sharpening Python skills
  with a deadline.
